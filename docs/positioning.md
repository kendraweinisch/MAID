# End of Life Atlas: positioning

## Why it exists

People need a single place to get all the information about end-of-life
planning, in a nonjudgmental, international tone that respects each person's
right to die with dignity.

## The promise

**Every part of the end of life, explained plainly, for where you live.**

From planning ahead, through serious illness and the last weeks, to what to do
after a death and how to grieve. Medical aid in dying is covered as one choice
among many, with the same care as the rest.

## Who it's for

- Adults planning ahead (often prompted by a parent's decline or a diagnosis)
- Families caring for someone who is seriously ill
- People who have just lost someone and need to know what to do next
- The professionals who support them: hospice staff, social workers, doulas, chaplains

## What we take from competitors, and what we add

| Competitor | Their best part | Our version |
|---|---|---|
| Cake | Huge library of plain articles; free planning profile | Fewer, deeper guides, organized by stage, with dates and sources |
| Everplans | State guides; a place to record what matters | Printable worksheets, no account needed |
| Empathy | Step-by-step help after a death | A free "first 24 hours" and "first month" checklist |
| After Party | Warmth and personality; children's grief workbooks; printables | The same warmth; grief guides for children and pets |
| Ever Loved, Meadow | Price transparency for funerals and cremation | Your rights under the FTC Funeral Rule, and every disposition option compared |
| Compassion & Choices, Death with Dignity | Accurate state-by-state aid-in-dying information | The same accuracy, kept current, inside a wider guide |

## What makes us different

1. **Organized by stage, not by product.** "Where are you right now?" is the first question, not "sign up."
2. **Place-specific.** The long-term goal is a page per state and country covering every end-of-life rule: advance directive forms, POLST, aid in dying, funeral and burial laws, probate thresholds.
3. **Verified and dated.** Every factual page shows when it was last checked and links its sources.
4. **Nothing to sell you inside the guide.** No ads, no referral fees, no funeral-home leads.
5. **Covers the hard topics plainly,** including medical aid in dying, which planning apps avoid.

## Funding

None. End of Life Atlas is a voluntary, information-only site with no business
model, no advertising, no sponsorship, no referral fees and nothing for sale.
That independence is part of why readers can trust it. Earlier ideas for paid
navigation, licensing and training have been dropped.

## Content roadmap

1. Now: stage guides, three printable checklists, aid-in-dying section with 23 places.
2. Next: state pages for advance directives and POLST (official forms linked).
3. Then: funeral and burial rules by state (home funerals, green burial, cremation).
4. Then: probate and small-estate thresholds by state.
5. Ongoing: grief guides for children (printable), pets, and anticipatory grief.
